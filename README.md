## Overview

A laboratory gas delivery system transports gases from cylinders,
cylinder banks, bulk sources or generators to the instruments and
processes that use them. A typical system combines source equipment,
changeover manifolds, pressure regulation, distribution tubing,
isolation components and point-of-use connections.

LGS provides equipment and project services across this gas path,
including system design, production, installation, testing,
commissioning and documentation.

## Typical Gas Delivery Path

A laboratory gas system commonly follows these stages:

1.  **Gas source** --- cylinders, cylinder packs, bulk tanks, generators
    or gas cabinets.
2.  **Changeover** --- switching between duty and reserve sources where
    required.
3.  **Pressure regulation** --- reducing and controlling pressure for
    the distribution network.
4.  **Distribution** --- transporting gas through compatible tubing,
    joints and isolation valves.
5.  **Point of use** --- final regulation and connection to an
    instrument or process.
6.  **Safety systems** --- suitable monitoring, ventilation interfaces,
    alarms and shutoff arrangements.

The actual configuration must be selected for the gas properties, hazard
classification, required purity, pressure, flow and applicable project
requirements.

## Design and Safety Considerations

Gas systems are safety-critical installations. Design and equipment
selection should be completed by qualified professionals using
project-specific information.

Important considerations include:

-   Gas identity, properties and compatibility
-   Hazard classification and segregation of incompatible gases
-   Required purity, pressure and flow
-   Wetted materials, tubing finish and connection method
-   Ventilation, gas detection and emergency isolation
-   Inspection, maintenance and accessibility
-   Pressure testing, leak testing and purging
-   Drawings, material records and commissioning documentation

This repository is an introductory technical overview, not an
installation manual or a substitute for engineering design, applicable
codes or site-specific safety procedures.
